What drives churn

Findings from exploratory analysis of all 7,043 customers.

  • Contract is the strongest signal. Month-to-month customers churn at 42.7%, one-year at 11.3%, two-year at 2.8%.
  • The first year is the danger zone. Customers with tenure of 12 months or less churn at 47.4%, later customers at 17.1%.
  • Fiber optic customers churn at 41.9%, DSL at 19.0%, no internet at 7.4%. Association, not proof of cause.

From scores to a business decision

A retention offer costs $30 per contacted customer, saves 30% of those who would really leave, and a saved customer is worth 12 months of their bill (assumptions, editable in the code). Contacting customers above a churn probability of 0.14 gives about $67.8k expected profit on the test set versus $55.7k for contacting everyone, at 91% recall and 44% precision.

Limitations

  • Public sample data with no dates, so no time-based validation.
  • Patterns are correlations. The data cannot show that a factor causes churn.
  • The profit figures depend on assumed costs. They show the method, not a business case.
